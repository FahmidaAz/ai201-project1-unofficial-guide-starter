CODING INTERVIEW PREPARATION GUIDE — From Engineers Who've Been Through It

HOW LONG DOES IT TAKE TO PREPARE?
Most people need 2-4 months of consistent practice to be ready for FAANG-level interviews. For mid-level companies, 4-6 weeks is usually enough if you already have a CS foundation. Don't rush — it's better to delay applying than to bomb interviews at your dream company.

WHAT TO STUDY
Focus on these data structures and algorithms in order:
1. Arrays and Strings
2. Hash Maps and Sets
3. Two Pointers
4. Sliding Window
5. Linked Lists
6. Stacks and Queues
7. Binary Search
8. Trees and BST
9. Graphs (BFS, DFS)
10. Dynamic Programming (last, hardest)

HOW MANY LEETCODE PROBLEMS DO YOU NEED?
Quality over quantity. 100-150 problems done with deep understanding beats 300 problems memorized. After solving a problem, always ask: what is the pattern here? Can I apply this pattern elsewhere?

THE BLIND 75
The "Blind 75" is a curated list of 75 LeetCode problems that cover all major patterns. Many engineers swear by this list. Solving all 75 with full understanding is better than randomly grinding 500 problems.

PRACTICE TALKING OUT LOUD
Silent coding in interviews is a red flag. Practice explaining your thought process as you code. Say things like: "I'm thinking about using a hash map here because lookup is O(1)" or "My first approach is brute force O(n^2), but I think we can optimize this."

THE INTERVIEW FORMAT AT MOST COMPANIES
- 5 minutes: introductions and small talk
- 5 minutes: clarify the problem (ask questions!)
- 30 minutes: solve the problem while explaining
- 5 minutes: analyze time and space complexity
- 5 minutes: questions for the interviewer

ALWAYS CLARIFY BEFORE CODING
Before writing a single line of code, ask:
- What are the input constraints?
- Can the input be empty or null?
- Should I handle negative numbers?
- Is there a memory constraint?
This shows maturity and prevents solving the wrong problem.

TIME AND SPACE COMPLEXITY
You must be able to analyze Big O for every solution. Practice this for every problem you solve. Interviewers always ask. Common complexities to know: O(1), O(log n), O(n), O(n log n), O(n^2), O(2^n).

BEHAVIORAL QUESTIONS (STAR FORMAT)
Use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
Prepare stories for: a time you failed, a conflict with a coworker, your biggest achievement, a time you led a project, a time you disagreed with your manager.

SYSTEM DESIGN INTERVIEWS
For senior roles, you'll get system design questions like "Design Twitter" or "Design a URL shortener." Study: load balancing, databases (SQL vs NoSQL), caching, message queues, CDNs, and horizontal vs vertical scaling.
