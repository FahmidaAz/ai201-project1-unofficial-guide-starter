WHAT TO DO AFTER A JOB REJECTION — How to Bounce Back and Keep Going

REJECTION IS NORMAL — THE NUMBERS DON'T LIE
Even strong candidates get rejected 80-90% of the time. A software engineer with 3 years of experience might apply to 50 companies, get 15 phone screens, 5 technical interviews, and 2 offers. Rejection is not a sign you're unqualified — it's a statistical reality of the process.

IMMEDIATELY AFTER REJECTION
1. Give yourself 24 hours to feel disappointed. It's okay and normal.
2. Don't send an angry or defensive reply.
3. Send a gracious thank-you: "Thank you for letting me know. I enjoyed the process and hope to stay in touch. Could you share any feedback on areas to improve?"
(Most companies won't give feedback due to legal reasons, but it doesn't hurt to ask.)

ASK FOR FEEDBACK (POLITELY)
Some recruiters will share feedback. When they do, it's gold. Common feedback categories:
- Technical skills: "Your data structures were strong but system design needs work"
- Communication: "We'd like to see clearer thought process during problem solving"
- Culture fit: (Usually vague and not actionable)
If you get specific feedback, prioritize fixing that exact thing before your next interview.

REAPPLYING TO THE SAME COMPANY
Most big tech companies have a waiting period of 6-12 months before you can reapply. Use that time to genuinely improve. Many engineers get hired on their second or third attempt. Getting rejected by Google once doesn't mean never — it means not yet.

DIAGNOSING WHERE YOU FAILED
- Rejected before phone screen: Resume or ATS keyword issue
- Rejected after recruiter screen: Communication, enthusiasm, or background gaps
- Rejected after technical phone screen: Coding skills need work
- Rejected after onsite: Either technical depth or behavioral fit

BUILDING A PIPELINE TO SURVIVE REJECTION
Don't put all your energy into one company. Apply to 10-15 companies simultaneously. Create a spreadsheet tracking: company, role, date applied, stage, follow-up date. Treat job searching like a numbers game — more at-bats means more chances.

MENTAL HEALTH DURING JOB SEARCH
Long job searches (3-6+ months) are emotionally exhausting. Common advice from engineers who've been through it:
- Set a daily limit: apply to 5 jobs per day, then stop for the day
- Celebrate small wins: a recruiter screen is a win worth acknowledging
- Talk to others going through the same thing: Reddit r/cscareerquestions, Discord communities
- Take breaks from applying: one day per week with no job search activity
- Separate your worth from the outcome: a rejection is not a judgment of your value as a person

USING REJECTION TO IMPROVE
After every failed technical interview, write down every problem you couldn't solve. Add them to your practice list. Study the solutions. Rejection interviews are actually the most valuable study sessions — you know exactly what you need to work on.

WHEN TO CONSIDER ALTERNATIVE PATHS
If you've applied to 100+ jobs with no offers after 6 months:
- Consider a lower-level role to get experience (junior, associate, QA, data analyst)
- Consider smaller companies and startups to get your first job
- Consider contract work or freelance projects to build your portfolio
- Get honest feedback on your resume and portfolio from experienced engineers
- Consider if there's a skill gap that needs to be filled first

YOU WILL GET A JOB
Almost every engineer who keeps improving and keeps applying eventually gets a job. The ones who don't make it are the ones who give up. The job search is a test of persistence as much as technical skill.
