TECH RESUME TIPS — Advice from Engineers and Hiring Managers

KEEP IT TO ONE PAGE
Most recruiters spend less than 30 seconds on a resume. Keep it to one page unless you have 10+ years of experience. Cut anything older than 5 years if you're running out of space.

USE A SIMPLE FORMAT
Avoid tables, columns, graphics, and icons. Many companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) that can't parse fancy layouts. Stick to plain text with clear section headers: Education, Experience, Projects, Skills.

QUANTIFY EVERYTHING
Bad: "Improved website performance"
Good: "Reduced page load time by 40% by implementing lazy loading and caching, improving user retention by 15%"
Numbers make your impact concrete and memorable.

TAILOR YOUR RESUME FOR EACH JOB
Read the job description carefully. Mirror the exact keywords they use. If they say "distributed systems" and your resume says "cloud infrastructure," update it. ATS filters by keyword match.

PROJECTS MATTER MORE THAN GPA
For new grads and bootcamp graduates, a strong projects section beats a high GPA. Show 2-3 projects with: what it does, what stack you used, and a GitHub link. Deployed projects are even better.

GITHUB LINK IS MANDATORY
Every tech resume should have a GitHub link. Recruiters and engineers will check it. Make sure your pinned repos have README files that explain the project clearly.

SKILLS SECTION
List your languages, frameworks, tools, and databases. Be honest — if you put it on your resume, expect to be asked about it in depth. Don't list something you Googled once.

AVOID THESE COMMON MISTAKES
- Listing "Microsoft Word" or "Google Docs" as skills
- Using an unprofessional email address
- Including a photo (not standard in the US)
- Listing references as "available upon request"
- Using passive voice: "Was responsible for" → "Built", "Led", "Designed"

ACTION VERBS TO USE
Built, Designed, Implemented, Optimized, Reduced, Increased, Led, Deployed, Automated, Refactored, Integrated, Migrated, Architected, Shipped

EDUCATION SECTION
List your degree, school, graduation year, and GPA (if above 3.5). For bootcamp graduates, list the bootcamp with completion date and any relevant certifications.

THE BEST RESUME FORMAT FOR NEW GRADS
1. Name + Contact Info + GitHub + LinkedIn
2. Education
3. Technical Skills
4. Projects (2-3 with descriptions)
5. Experience (internships, part-time, freelance)
6. Certifications (optional)

HOW TO GET FEEDBACK
Post your resume on Reddit r/cscareerquestions with the "Resume Advice" flair. Remove personal info first. The community gives detailed, honest feedback. Blind app also has a resume review section.

COVER LETTERS
Most tech companies don't read cover letters. Focus your energy on the resume and portfolio. The exception is small startups and companies that explicitly ask for one.
