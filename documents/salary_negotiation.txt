SALARY NEGOTIATION FOR TECH JOBS — How to Get Paid What You're Worth

NEVER ACCEPT THE FIRST OFFER
The first offer is almost never the best offer. Companies expect negotiation. Not negotiating is leaving money on the table — sometimes $10,000-$30,000 per year.

KNOW YOUR MARKET VALUE FIRST
Before negotiating, research salaries at:
- levels.fyi (best for total compensation at tech companies)
- glassdoor.com
- linkedin.com/salary
- blind app (anonymous self-reported salaries)
Know the base salary, bonus, and equity for your role, level, and location.

TOTAL COMPENSATION VS BASE SALARY
At big tech companies, total comp includes:
- Base salary
- Annual bonus (10-20% of base)
- RSUs/Stock (vests over 4 years, often with 1-year cliff)
A $150k base with $50k/year in stock is a $200k total comp offer. Always compare total comp, not just base.

HOW TO NEGOTIATE
Email is better than phone. It gives you time to think and creates a paper trail. Say something like: "I'm very excited about this opportunity. Based on my research of market rates and my experience, I was hoping we could get closer to [X amount]. Is there flexibility there?"

COMPETING OFFERS ARE YOUR BEST LEVERAGE
If you have another offer, use it. You don't have to name the company, but say: "I have a competing offer at $X. I'd prefer to join your company but need the compensation to be closer to that range." Companies will often match or beat competing offers.

WHAT YOU CAN NEGOTIATE BEYOND BASE SALARY
- Signing bonus (easiest to negotiate, comes from different budget)
- RSU grant size
- Accelerated vesting schedule
- Remote work flexibility
- Start date
- Title/level (affects all future comp)

NEVER GIVE A NUMBER FIRST
When asked "What are your salary expectations?" say: "I'm flexible and more focused on finding the right fit. What is the budgeted range for this role?" If pressed, give a range based on your research, not what you "need."

IF THEY SAY THE OFFER IS FINAL
It rarely is. You can always say: "I understand. Can we revisit the signing bonus or equity then?" HR has different budgets for different components.

NEGOTIATING AS A NEW GRAD
New grad offers at big tech are often banded — base may be fixed by level. Focus on: signing bonus, RSU refreshers, and level (L3 vs L4 can mean $30k+ difference in total comp).

AFTER YOU ACCEPT
Once you accept verbally or in writing, negotiation is over. Don't try to renegotiate after signing. Focus on performing well to get strong performance bonuses and raises.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Accepting immediately without thinking
- Apologizing for negotiating
- Revealing your current salary (illegal to ask in some states)
- Negotiating against yourself by lowballing
- Not negotiating at all out of fear of losing the offer (this almost never happens)
